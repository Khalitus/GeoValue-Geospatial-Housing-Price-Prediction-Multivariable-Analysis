Methods and research boundaries

How to interpret GeoValue.

The dataset, evaluation protocol, spatial assumptions and limits are part of the result.

01 / Data

Official county transaction records

Residential valid sales from 2013–2025 are joined with parcel assessments and the March 2025 centroids. These are transaction-level prices, not current asking prices.

02 / Evaluation

Chronological and parcel-disjoint

Training uses sales through 2023, validation uses 2024, and the held-out test contains 2025. A parcel identifier cannot appear across those partitions; municipality holdout provides an additional geographical test.

03 / Model

XGBoost selected by validation RMSE

Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost use common leakage-conscious preprocessing. The winning architecture is refitted on training plus validation records before final testing.

04 / Spatial evidence

Observed density, not confidence

The 10th-nearest-training-location distance determines dense, typical and sparse local support categories. Error statistics are calibrated on 2024 validation transactions, with an untouched 2025 support audit. Neither the category nor the 80th-percentile historical error guarantees coverage for a new property.

05 / Calculus

Smoothed controlled surface

Housing attributes are fixed while projected coordinates vary. Gaussian smoothing makes finite-difference gradients and numerical area integration interpretable as properties of the scenario surface—not physical appreciation or causal changes.

06 / Limitations

Retrospective assessment information

The contemporary property assessment may describe improvements that occurred after past transactions. Sale records are nominal dollars across years; the numerical integration region can extend outside county land. This work is not an appraisal.
